# Criteria Map — EB-1A and O-1

Verify any citation against the current regulation and USCIS Policy Manual before relying on it
in a filing. Regulations and policy change; this file is a working map, not authority.

---

## EB-1A — 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3)

Either a one-time major internationally recognized award, **or at least three** of:

| Reg. | Criterion |
|---|---|
| (i) | Lesser nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence |
| (ii) | Membership in associations requiring outstanding achievement, judged by recognized experts |
| (iii) | Published material about the person in professional or major trade/media, relating to their work |
| (iv) | Participation as a judge of the work of others in the field |
| (v) | Original contributions of major significance to the field |
| (vi) | Authorship of scholarly articles in the field |
| (vii) | Display of work at artistic exhibitions or showcases |
| (viii) | Leading or critical role for organizations with a distinguished reputation |
| (ix) | High salary or remuneration relative to others in the field |
| (x) | Commercial success in the performing arts |

**Order matters when you write.** Organize the brief by these Roman numerals with the mapping
table **at the front**, not by thematic headings. An adjudicator who has to map your evidence to
the regulation themselves will resolve ambiguity against the petitioner. Do not renumber the
criteria into your own scheme.

### The two-step analysis

Under *Kazarian v. USCIS*, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010):

**Step one — counting.** Does the evidence meet at least three criteria? Officers may not import
extra requirements that are not in the regulation at this stage.

**Step two — final merits determination.** Taking the record as a whole, does it show sustained
national or international acclaim and that the person is among the small percentage at the very
top of the field?

**Meeting three criteria does not win the case.** A review that only checks step one has done
half the job. Always run a separate step-two pass asking what the *totality* conveys.

## O-1A — 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iii)

Extraordinary ability in science, education, business, or athletics. A major internationally
recognized award, **or at least three** of the listed forms of evidence — nationally or
internationally recognized prizes; membership in associations requiring outstanding achievement;
published material about the person; participation as a judge; original contributions of major
significance; authorship of scholarly articles; critical or essential capacity for organizations
with a distinguished reputation; high salary or remuneration.

**O-1B** (arts, motion picture and television) uses a different standard and its own criteria at
8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(3)(iv) — do not apply O-1A criteria to an O-1B filing.

**Additional O-1 requirements that are easy to miss and are frequently fatal:**

- **Consultation / advisory opinion** from an appropriate peer group, labor organization, or
  management organization — 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(5). Check it exists, is current, and is from the
  right body.
- **Employer or agent petitioner.** O-1 is not self-petitioned the way EB-1A is. Confirm the
  petitioner relationship and the itinerary/contract requirements are actually satisfied.
- **Defined period of activity** — the event, project, or engagement must be specified.

**Overlap note:** O-1A evidence and EB-1A evidence overlap heavily, but the standards differ and
an approved O-1 does not establish EB-1A eligibility. Never argue that it does.

## Disposition rules

- **Every criterion gets a disposition.** Claimed, or expressly not relied upon. Never silent.
- **One field of endeavor**, phrased identically everywhere.
- **Do not double-use a person across criteria.** The same recommender appearing under both
  judging and original contributions weakens both and looks like padding.
- **Lead with the strongest criterion**, not the one with the most exhibits.
- **Demote arguments that adjudicators have rejected** to defensive footnotes rather than
  leading with them.
- **Watch recency.** Evidence clustered years in the past invites a "sustained acclaim" attack.
  Either add current-era evidence or address the gap explicitly.
