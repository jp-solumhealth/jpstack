# Consistency Audit

The highest-yield pass in the whole review. Most avoidable denials are not weak evidence — they
are a record that contradicts itself.

## Why it is decisive

Under *Matter of Ho*, 19 I&N Dec. 582 (BIA 1988), it is **incumbent upon the petitioner to
resolve inconsistencies in the record by independent objective evidence**; explanations that
merely attempt to reconcile the conflict, without objective evidence showing where the truth
lies, will not suffice.

Read that as an operating rule: once an officer finds one contradiction, the cost is not limited
to the contradicted fact. Doubt spreads to the rest of the record, and the burden to clean it up
falls on the petitioner — and an RFE deadline cannot be extended (8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(8)(iv)).

Independently, a support letter that conflicts with other evidence may be given **less weight**
(*Matter of Caron International, Inc.*, 19 I&N Dec. 791 (BIA 1988)). A contradictory letter is
not neutral. It is a liability.

## The seven checks

Run all seven. Quote both sides of every conflict verbatim — never paraphrase a contradiction,
because the paraphrase is where the conflict quietly disappears.

**1. Entity and corporate history.** Every company name, founding date, acquisition, rebrand,
accelerator batch, and corporate-continuity claim, across the brief *and* every letter. Where a
person's career spans multiple entities, fix one canonical sentence describing the relationship
and use it verbatim everywhere. **This is the single most common serious defect** and it is
trivially checkable by an officer.

**2. Numbers against their own exhibits.** Take every figure in the brief — market size, revenue,
headcount, users, funding, rankings, acceptance rates — and check it against the exhibit it
cites *and* against every other document in the packet. A brief that states a figure its own
support letters contradict has damaged itself. Flag any statistic with no exhibit as UNSUPPORTED.

**3. Third-party statistics.** Any externally verifiable claim — application counts, portfolio
sizes, selection rates, market figures, "first" or "only" claims. Superlatives ("the only X",
"the first Y") deserve the harshest scrutiny: they are the easiest to disprove and the most
damaging when disproved. Verify against a primary source or cut the claim.

**4. Signatories.** Does the person the brief names as signing a document match the person who
actually signed it? Do titles, affiliations, and dates match? Does the signer plausibly have
first-hand knowledge of what they attest to?

**5. Evidence versus argument.** Does the exhibit prove what the brief says it proves? A letter
describing participation in a *program* does not establish selection as a *member*. A press
mention is not published material *about* the person. An advisory role is not a *leading or
critical* role. This mismatch is invisible to the drafter and obvious to the officer.

**6. Dates and chronology.** Filing dates, employment dates, award dates, letter dates,
placeholders left unfilled. Check that no letter postdates the event it describes implausibly and
that no template placeholder survived into the final draft.

**7. Internal cross-references.** Do exhibit citations point at the exhibit that actually exists?
Does the exhibit index match the exhibits? Do paragraph cross-references resolve? Broken
references read as carelessness and invite closer reading of everything else.

## Output format per finding

```
[P1] [CONTRADICTION] Corporate continuity
  Brief ¶NN:      "<quote>"
  Exhibit K-N:    "<quote>"
  Why:            Officer-verifiable in seconds; triggers Matter of Ho burden across record.
  Fix:            Adopt canonical phrasing "<X>"; reissue or footnote the conflicting letter.
  Needs:          [NEEDS SOURCE: confirmation of <fact> from <who>]
```

## Priority

- **P1** — can sink the petition alone: an officer-verifiable contradiction, a false or
  unsupportable statement, a missing required element (e.g. an O-1 consultation), an undecided
  criterion.
- **P2** — materially weakens a criterion: evidence/argument mismatch, unsupported figure,
  double-used recommender, stale evidence with no bridge.
- **P3** — polish: cross-references, formatting, ordering, grammar.

Fix every P1 before touching a single P3. Ranking by leverage is where the value of this review
actually lives.
